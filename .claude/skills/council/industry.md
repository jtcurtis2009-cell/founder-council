## 1. STR management business model

**Fees**
- **Full-service fees:** 20–40% of rental revenue, with most managers between 20% and 30% [E] ([RedAwning](https://www.redawning.com/vacation-rentals/pm/post/vacation-rental-management-fees)). Other blogs give 20–35% [E] ([Skyrun](https://skyrun.com/blog/faq/what-is-a-typical-vacation-rental-management-fee/)), and one guide says around 25%, ranging from 15% to 40% [E] (cited in [Awning review](https://awning.com/post/awning-property-management-review)).
- **Half-service:** averages 10–15% [E] ([RedAwning](https://www.redawning.com/vacation-rentals/pm/post/how-to-set-vacation-rental-management-fees-pricing-guide)).
- **Co-hosting:** usually 10–20% [E] ([Zeevou](https://zeevou.com/blog/airbnb-co-hosting-fee/)). Other sources give 15–30% of the booking subtotal [E] ([Houst](https://www.houst.com/blog/how-much-do-airbnb-co-hosts-earn)). Full-service co-listing is 20–25% or more [E] ([Boostly](https://boostly.co.uk/airbnb-co-listing-guide/)). No survey average exists.
- **Airbnb's host-only fee is 15.5%,** mandatory for PMS-connected US/Canadian hosts from 2025. Sources disagree on the date (13 Apr vs 27 Oct 2025) [CR via blogs]. [Hostfully](https://www.hostfully.com/blog/airbnb-host-fees/). This fee comes out of the gross before the PM's cut.
- **Named operators:**
  - Evolve: 10% Core, 15% Plus [CR] ([RedAwning review](https://redawning.com/pm/post/evolve-property-management-review-2026)).
  - Awning: "starts at 15%". Competitor pages claim 15–20% [CR/E] ([Awning](https://awning.com/post/awning-property-management-review), [Tidy](https://www.tidy.com/comparisons/awning)). RedAwning acquired Awning in Apr 2024 [CR].
  - Vacasa: did not disclose a take rate. Third parties estimate 25–35% [E] ([Awning review](https://awning.com/post/vacasa-property-management-review)).

**Unit economics**
- **San Diego STR revenue:** about $36.4k–$39.2k per unit per year [S] ([AirDNA](https://www.airdna.co/vacation-rental-data/app/us/california/san-diego)). At a 25% fee, that is roughly **$9–10k of fee revenue per unit** [E: 0.25 × $36–39k]. At 2,000 STR units, that would be about $18–20M of fee revenue [E].
- **Vacasa gross revenue per home:** about $23k in 2024 [E: $910.5M ÷ ~39k average homes]. This figure includes guest fees Vacasa books gross, so it overstates its "fee" [CR inputs, [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1874944/000187494425000003/vcsa-20241231.htm)]. GBV per night sold was $365 [CR].
- **Gross margin (hybrid PMC):** under 30% needs work, 30–45% is acceptable and over 45% is exceptional [E, podcast benchmark] ([STR Global Unlocked](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2539584/episodes/19353903-the-margin-mistake-killing-most-str-companies)).
- **Net margin:** sources disagree, between 5–12% "acceptable" (most PMCs <5%) [E] (same source) and 20–30% for the largest respondents [S, Uplisting survey] ([Uplisting](https://www.uplisting.io/blog/how-much-money-can-you-actually-make-with-short-term-rentals)).

**Churn and CAC**
- **Owner churn:** no independent survey rate was found. Vacasa lost about 5% of homeowners in 2023 [CR via [Skift](https://skift.com/2024/02/29/can-vacasas-strategy-change-keep-homeowners-from-leaving/)], then about 4,000 homes year on year by Q2-24 [CR via press]. The drivers were rates and revenue, and owner communication [Skift].
- **Owner CAC:** no per-owner dollar figure is published.
  - Vacasa targeted a 4–5x LTV:CAC ratio for individual-home acquisition in 2021 [CR] ([RentalScaleUp](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/vacasa-vcsa-2021-results/)).
  - It capitalized $29.6M (2024) and $34.8M (2023) of contract-acquisition costs [CR] ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1874944/000187494425000003/vcsa-20241231.htm)).
  - The TurnKey roll-up booked $102.3M of homeowner-contract intangibles, amortized over 5 years [CR] (same).
  - One vendor model puts a lost owner at up to $246k of revenue over 8.3 years [E, vendor] ([Hostaway glossary](https://www.hostaway.com/glossary/owner-churn/) / Properly).

**Industry context**
- **AirDNA 2025 forecast (US):** demand +4.9%, supply +4.7%, RevPAR +2.9% [S] ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241205094869/en/AirDNA-2025-Outlook-Report-U.S.-Short-Term-Rental-Industry-Finds-Balance)). AirDNA's 2026 outlook expects occupancy to decline slightly [S] ([AirDNA](https://www.airdna.co/outlook-report-2024)).
- **KeyData:** 2024 and 2025 annual figures were "pacing almost identically" [S] ([KeyData SOTI 2025](https://assets.simpleviewinc.com/simpleview/image/upload/v1/clients/outerbanks/KeyData_State_of_the_Industry_2025_8aa3490a-5629-4de2-ac8f-98779f9ee455.pdf)).
- **Professional manager share:** about 69% of US units [S, PriceLabs/Rentals United] ([ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/rentals-united-pricelabs-data/)).
- **Team size:** 74% of PMs run with ≤3 people [S, PriceLabs, >750 PMs] ([PriceLabs](https://hello.pricelabs.co/es/informe-property-managers/)).
- **Evolve:** raised about $224–235M, including a $100M Series F in 2022. It reported 19k+ homes in 2022 and "30k+ owners" by 2024 [CR/E]. Owners and homes are not comparable counts ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/dossier/evolve-funding), [sp-edge](https://sp-edge.com/companies/223548)).
- **AvantStay** (owner-hosted luxury): raised a $160M Series B and a $500M PropCo round (2021–22), then made three layoff rounds in 12 months, one of them 22% of staff [CR via press]. CB Insights lists a Series C dated May 2025 with no amount [E] ([ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/avantstay-third-round-redundancies/), [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/avantstay)).

## 3. Boutique hotel economics

**Management contract fees**
- **Base fee:** 2–4% of total revenue, most often 3% [S] ([HVS](https://www.hvs.com/article/9912-A-New-Approach-to-Hotel-Management-Fees); older [HVS 2011](https://www.hvs.com/Content/3173.pdf): 2–3%).
- **Incentive fee:** about 8–10% of GOP is "standard". A 3% base plus 3% incentive is about 8% of GOP at a 37.5% margin [E, HVS founder commentary] ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4052689)).
- **REIT reality:** DiamondRock's contracts carry a 1.5–3% base and 10–25% incentive, some with caps [CR] ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1298946/000129894625000015/drh-20241231.htm)).
- **Brand key money:** often $2k–$10k+ per key [E] (cited in [Hotel Management](https://hotelmanagement.mydigitalpublication.com/articles/spark-by-hilton-mystic-conn-groton)).

**Benchmarks**
- **US hotels, 2025:** occupancy 62.3% (−1.2%), ADR $160.54 (+0.9%), RevPAR $100.02 (−0.3%). This was the first full-year occupancy and RevPAR decline since 2020. The 2024 figures were 63.0%, $158.67 and $99.94 [S, CoStar] ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/press-releases/us-hotels-report-first-full-year-occupancy-revpar)).
- **Hotel margins:** GOP margin was 34.8% in 2025 (35.1% in 2024) and EBITDA margin 22.8% (23.3%) [S, CBRE] ([Lodging](https://lodgingmagazine.com/profitability-under-pressure-how-2025-u-s-hotel-performance-reshapes-valuation-underwriting/)). HotelData YTD Sep-25 GOP margin was 37.7%, and Q4-25 was 36.0% [S] ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4134371/not-done-weekly-the-margin-crunch-in-hotels-continues)).
- **San Diego County, mid-2025 (CoStar):**
  - Year to date: occupancy 71.5%, ADR $204.31, RevPAR $146.12 [S].
  - Trailing 12 months: 74.0%, $214.49, $158.72 [S].
  - Carlsbad/Oceanside ADR and RevPAR were up, while Mission Valley RevPAR fell 2.0% [S] ([Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/press_releases/release/san-diego-mid-year-2025-hotel-market-report-adr-revpar-occupancy-by-submarket-robert-rauch/)).
  - A late-2025 forecast expected full-year 2025 RevPAR to go negative on new supply [S] ([Hotel-Online 2026 forecast](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/2026-lodging-forecast-san-diego)).
- **San Diego supply:** about 66,500 metro rooms existing, with about 2,900 under construction (Jan 2025) [S, CoStar] ([CoStar](https://costar.com/article/849282717/san-diego-hotel-pipeline-is-among-largest-of-the-top-25-markets)). By mid-2025, 1,400 rooms were under construction (+2.1% inventory), including the 1,600-room Gaylord in Chula Vista, which had opened [S] ([Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/press_releases/release/san-diego-mid-year-2025-hotel-market-report-adr-revpar-occupancy-by-submarket-robert-rauch/)).

**Hotel vs STR revenue per unit, for the manager [E, my arithmetic from the sourced inputs above]**
- **Hotel key:** San Diego RevPAR of $158.72 × 365 is about $57.9k of room revenue per key per year. A 3% base fee is about **$1.7k per key**. Adding an incentive fee of 10% of GOP at a 35% margin adds about **$2.0k**. Total: about **$3.7k per key per year**, before any F&B revenue.
- **STR unit:** about $36–39k of revenue × 25% fee is about **$9–10k per unit per year** to the manager.
- **What this means:** a managed hotel key earns about **⅓ to ½ of an STR unit's fee**. But a single contract adds 20–100 keys at once, there is no STRO license cap, and the work is staffed on site rather than scattered.

**Small hotel and motel conversion [E, mostly vendor and brand figures]**
- Hilton expects Spark conversions to cost $18k–$23k per key, and a deep conversion reached $35k per key ([Hotel Management](https://hotelmanagement.mydigitalpublication.com/articles/spark-by-hilton-mystic-conn-groton)).
- Vendor guides put a full renovation at $25k–$60k per key, and an upscale repositioning at $60k–$120k+ ([PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q13796)).
- A 27-key motel bought for $2.45M had $500k of renovation, about $18.5k per key.
- A Denver motel-to-boutique conversion was a $22M project for 54 rooms, about $407k per key all-in ([WhatNow](https://whatnow.com/denver/restaurants/all-inn-motel-transforming-into-boutique-hotel)).
- Ground-up select-service development had a median of about $223k per room in 2025 [S, HVS, cited in same results].

## 4. San Diego / North County regulation

**City of San Diego (STRO, in effect since 2023)**
- **Tier 1:** part-time (≤20 days a year). **Tier 2:** home-share. Neither is capped.
- **Tier 3:** whole-home rentals for more than 20 days a year, **capped at 1% of city housing stock**.
  - The city page (Aug 2026) shows **4,836 issued and 829 remaining** [CR] ([sandiego.gov/stro](https://sandiego.gov/stro)).
  - A 2020 city document put the cap at about **5,419** [CR] ([memo](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsd_pc-20-064_memorandum.pdf)).
  - Third-party figures (5,551) conflict, so don't use them ([bnbcalc](https://www.bnbcalc.com/es/blog/short-term-rental-regulation/san-diego-california-guide)).
- **Tier 4:** Mission Beach whole-home, capped at **30% of local housing, about 1,081 units**. The application period and waitlist are closed. When applications exceed the cap, licenses are allocated by lottery [CR] ([Times of SD](https://timesofsandiego.com/business/2022/11/24/nov-30-is-deadline-for-new-licenses-for-short-term-home-rentals-in-san-diego/)).
- **TOT (city):** historically 10.5%. Hotels with 70+ rooms pay an extra 2% TMD assessment [CR] ([SDBJ](https://www.sdbj.com/?p=42848), [City TOT](https://www.sandiego.gov/tot)).
  - A third-party site says Measure C replaced the flat rate from 1 May 2025 with zones of **11.75%, 12.75% and 13.75%**, set by distance from the Convention Center, and a 0.55% TMD for properties under 70 rooms [E, unverified against city] ([Roommaster](https://www.roommaster.com/hotel-tax/san-diego)).

**Where growth to 2,000 has to come from [E]**
- The city of San Diego has only about 5.4–5.7k Tier 3 licenses plus about 1,081 Tier 4, and almost all are taken (829 Tier 3 left).
- Oceanside's coastal non-hosted cap is 480, and Carlsbad allows STRs only in the coastal zone.
- So **new whole-home STR licenses in the core market are nearly fixed**. STR growth here has to come from taking existing licensed units from competitors (switching owners). Organic license growth won't get there.
- The rest has to come from **hotel keys (no STRO cap)**, 30+ day mid-term rentals, Tier 1/2 hosted units, or **other markets** (desert, mountain or out-of-state).

## 5. Growth channels

**Buying small PM companies**
- **Revenue multiples:** about 1–2.5x fee revenue for owner-operator shops [E] ([RedAwning](https://www.redawning.com/vacation-rentals/pm/post/vacation-rental-management-company-valuation)).
- **EBITDA multiples:** 2–3x adjusted EBITDA under $250k, and 3–5x above $500k [E] ([AvantStay](https://avantstay.com/blog/airbnb-portfolio-valuation-property-owners)). A searcher's own research gives 4–7x [E] ([SearchFunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/vacation-rental-management-valuation-multiples-benchmarks)).
- **What buyers discount for:** owner churn, trust-account problems and fragmented tech (up to −1.5x) [E].
- **Market:** Belcrest–TowneVacations, about $250M, is cited as the largest PM deal of 2026. About 200 US vacation rental managers with $2M+ profit remain independent [E] ([RedAwning](https://www.redawning.com/vacation-rentals/pm/post/private-equity-vacation-rental-consolidation-2026)).
- **Contract amortization:** Vacasa amortized acquired contracts over 5 years [CR], an implicit assumption about how long acquired owners stay.

**Referral and partner programs [CR, company pages]**
- **Flat fees:** $500 at SkyRun and Village Collective, $1,200 at Yellow Brick Road, and up to $1,500 at TurnKey ([SkyRun](https://skyrun.com/blog/skyrun-vacation-rentals-announces-worldwide-referral-program), [search results](https://business.winterpark.org/list/member/yellow-brick-road-homes-plus-5103953)).

## Lessons for getting to 2,000 units
1. **Don't sign master leases.** Sonder had $1.18B of lease liabilities and $303M a year in cash rent, and Selina had about $800M of liabilities. Both died in a soft market. Use management contracts, revenue share or franchise.
2. **Keep density local.** Vacasa's centralized roll-up lost about 6k homes in about 18 months. Casago won by putting local owner-operators back in charge and rehiring about 89% of field staff.
3. **Churn is the real growth rate.** A lost STR owner wipes out about $9–10k a year of fee revenue [E]. Track net units (adds minus losses) monthly, and tie owner reporting to revenue, which is the top churn driver.
4. **The San Diego STR license pool is capped.** There are about 5.4–5.7k Tier 3 plus about 1.1k Tier 4 licenses, with 829 Tier 3 left. Oceanside's coastal cap is 480 and Carlsbad is coastal-only. STR units here come mostly from switching owners away from other managers, so build switching offers (listing audits, revenue gap reports).
5. **Hotels are the scalable bucket.** One 40–80-key contract equals dozens of STR signings, with no STRO cap. But fees run about $3–4k per key against about $9–10k per STR unit [E], so price contracts with an incentive fee on GOP, not only a 3% base.
6. **Plan the mix to 2,000.** For example, about 600–900 STR units (taken from competitors plus North County) and about 1,100–1,400 hotel or aparthotel keys [E, illustrative split, not sourced]. The council should argue the exact split.
7. **Conversions are cheap per key, but capital-heavy.** Light motel or select-service conversions run about $18–35k per key, but all-in boutique repositioning can exceed $100k–$400k per key. Bring in owner or investor capital; don't use the PM company's balance sheet.
8. **Buy books at revenue multiples (about 1–2.5x fees) with earn-outs tied to owner retention.** Vacasa's 5-year amortization of acquired contracts and the 2023–24 owner exodus show acquired owners can leave.
9. **Pay referral partners.** $500–$1,500 flat fees or about 25% broker-to-broker fees are the market norm. Realtors are the natural channel at the moment an investor buys.
10. **Don't let tech claims outrun results.** Life House lost about a third of its owners trying to exit when its "tech-enabled" promises didn't show up in their returns. Underpromise to hotel owners.
11. **Grow direct bookings slowly and keep the OTA mix balanced.** Direct bookings are about 21–29% of professional-manager volume and slipping, while Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee squeezes the margin of PMS-connected managers.
12. **Price above Evolve's 10% anchor only with visible value.** Full-service fees of 20–30% need a revenue lift owners can see; otherwise they churn to cheaper models.
