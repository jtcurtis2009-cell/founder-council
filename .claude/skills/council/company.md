# Company profile: read before every debate

Every seat and the Chair read this file. Every recommendation must move the company toward the **North Star** without risking the company. Last updated with the founder's numbers on 2026-11-01. Lines marked *assumption* are best guesses, so correct them here.

## North Star
**Reach 2,000 units under management: a mix of boutique hotel rooms/keys and short-term rentals (STRs).**
- Target date: not set yet. **The council should recommend a realistic date.**
- Target mix: **60% STR / 40% hotels → about 1,200 STR units + 800 hotel keys.**
- Unit definition: 1 STR listing = 1 unit, and 1 hotel key = 1 unit (adjust if you count differently).

## Who we are
- **Company:** NOVIQ, a short-term rental property management company expanding into boutique hotels.
- **Home market:** San Diego and North County, California (Oceanside, Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach and nearby).
- **Founder / decision-maker:** Justin Curtis.
- **Today:** **236 units** under management (STR vs hotel-key split not given; *assumption:* mostly STR, with the hotel keys in the buildings below). That means about **8.5x growth** to reach 2,000.
- **Revenue model:** management fees of **20%** and **15%** of gross booking revenue (*assumption:* 20% full service, 15% for a lighter tier or larger portfolios). Other fees unknown.
- **Team:** **40 people, only 2 in sales.** Nearly all headcount is operations and delivery, so owner acquisition is a thin funnel staffed by 2 people.
- **Revenue:** about **$10M per year.** *Assumption:* this is **gross booking revenue** across our units (≈ $42k per unit per year, in line with San Diego STR averages in industry.md), which makes NOVIQ's fee revenue roughly **$1.5–2M per year** at 15–20%. If $10M is NOVIQ's own revenue instead, the company is about 5x larger than this assumes, so where it matters, show both cases.
- **Cash available for growth:** unknown. State assumptions.
- **Current owner churn per year:** unknown. Treat measuring it as a priority.
- **Revenue-increase guarantee:** we guarantee owners an increase in revenue. This is the core of our offer, and also a liability if we miss it, so the council should weigh how it is structured, priced and capped as we scale.

## How we win owners today
- **Primary focus: owner switchers.** This is the main growth channel.
- **Main avatar, the "switcher":** owners already running an STR with self-management or another company (e.g. Evolve, Vacasa) who are leaving revenue on the table. We prove the gap with a listing audit and a NOVIQ proposal: a boutique-hotel-style deck plus a pro forma.
- **Also:** owners not yet optimized (LTR/MTR) who could earn more as an STR or MTR.
- **Lead sources:** AirDNA sourcing of low-rated or newly active listings, STR license and permit lookups, skip tracing (Terrakotta), GoHighLevel nurture funnels, cold email/SMS/DM, LinkedIn outreach to multifamily owners and financial advisors, and referral partners.
- **Moving upmarket into luxury:** we want a bigger share of the luxury STR and boutique hotel market (higher nightly rates, higher-touch service, fewer but bigger units).
- **Acquisitions work for us:** we recently **acquired a small PM company with 21 units**. Buying small PM companies is a proven channel for us, not a theory.
- **Proof we sell on:** revenue uplift from dynamic pricing, better listings and reviews, and boutique-hotel-level guest experience.

## Boutique hotel strategy (fill in)

- Target property size and markets: not set. To reach ~800 hotel keys, the council should suggest a size range and markets, with a **luxury and wellness positioning** (see below).

## Strategic theme: wellness real estate
- The founder wants NOVIQ positioned in the **wellness real estate** trend: properties designed and operated around health (sleep, air and water quality, fitness, spa and recovery, nature, healthy food).
- Founder-provided market size: **about $1 trillion by 2030 and $1.8 trillion by 2035.** *Source not yet verified.* The Global Wellness Institute publishes wellness real estate forecasts, so check the exact figures and dates there before using them externally.
- Implications for the council to weigh: wellness-branded luxury STRs and boutique hotels (premium ADR, owner differentiation), wellness amenities as a reason for owners to switch, partnerships with wellness brands and practitioners, and which markets suit wellness travel (San Diego/North County coast, desert/spa markets).

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- San Diego STR licensing caps whole-home STR licenses (see industry.md), so STR growth inside the city is capped. Growth must come from North County, other markets, MTR, and hotels.
- Risk tolerance: no explicit debt or guarantee limits were given. The council should **recommend guardrails** (lease exposure, debt, guarantee liability) using the lessons in industry.md (Sonder, Selina, Vacasa).
- Brand standard: boutique-hotel-quality guest and owner experience at every unit.

- How do we move upmarket into luxury and wellness without breaking operations built for standard STRs?
- Should we repeat the 21-unit acquisition playbook? How often, and at what price (see industry.md multiples)?
