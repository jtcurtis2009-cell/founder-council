## Decision
Should our short-term rental management company grow by signing more individual STR owners or by taking on management of small boutique hotels?

**Context:** ~100 units under management in San Diego and North County, a lean team, and a goal of 2,000 units. The founder leaned toward C.
**Assumptions the Chair made (not given):** a 3–5 year horizon; a ~20–30% management fee on gross; bootstrapped funding; "small boutique hotel" means ~10–50 keys; the team has no prior hotel-ops experience.

## Options
- **A. Sign individual STR owners:** one owner, one unit at a time, with a repeatable acquisition machine.
- **B. Boutique hotel management:** more units per signature, but a different ops model.
- **C. Both, with a separate hotel team.**

## Option-by-option analysis
- **A:** *For:* you already deliver this offer, it can be turned into a playbook today, and referrals plus content make each new door cheaper. *Against:* ~1,900 signings needs a real acquisition engine, not founder-led sales.
- **B:** *For:* far more keys per signature from a less fragmented buyer pool. *Against:* no hotel-ops experience, a different customer, and concentration risk; one lost contract wipes out months of gains.
- **C:** *For:* keeps A running while testing the higher-leverage channel, and management contracts put no capital at risk. *Against:* a lean team running two businesses, with the founder as the de facto hotel team.

## Biggest risks to A, and how to de-risk them
1. **Acquisition doesn't scale past founder-led sales.** Add referral incentives, a content engine and a dedicated acquisition hire once the playbook exists.
2. **Missing the hotel window.** Keep a one-page hotel thesis and an operator-hire shortlist so you can move fast on a single pilot.
3. **Owner churn as volume grows.** Track owner NPS and revenue vs forecast per door every month.

## Conditions
1. Know your unit economics first (CAC per door, margin after cleaners and payroll, payback). Scale hard only if payback is short.
2. Turn onboarding and delivery into a playbook and get the founder out of daily delivery before 500 units.
3. Don't sign a hotel until a proven hotel operator owns that seat. The first one is a single pilot, not a separate team.

## Next 7 days
1. Pull 12 months of signings and build a CAC, margin and payback sheet per door.
2. Record the next owner onboarding end to end and turn it into an SOP.
3. Launch an owner referral bonus and a multi-property offer.
4. Start documenting owner wins as content.
5. Write a one-page hotel thesis covering target keys, fee model and the operator hire it would need.
